Creator’s Statement

Journeys of Knowledge

My creative project is an interactive website game designed to show how ideas about openness, authorship, and technical knowledge shaped early scientific practice. My research is based on Pamela Long’s chapter “Openness and Authorship I,” which explores how mining and metallurgy became written disciplines. The chapter changed the way I think about engineering because it shows that writing and knowledge sharing have always shaped how technical fields develop. The quotes I selected guided my design decisions and helped me build the message for my audience, which is first year engineering students who are still learning about what counts as engineering knowledge.

The first idea that shaped my project is Biringuccio’s belief that technical knowledge leads to more discovery when it is shared. Long quotes him directly by stating that “new information always gives birth in men’s mind to new discoveries and so to further information” (Long 179). Through this we can see that Biringuccio saw knowledge as something that grows when people communicate openly. My game uses this idea by giving players more progress and better outcomes when they choose actions that share information rather than hide it. The mechanic is designed around the quote itself. The player sees how openness creates new paths and possibilities, which reflects Biringuccio’s argument.

A second idea that shaped my project is Long’s claim that authorship changed technical work. She writes that “authorship created disciplines of knowledge out of practices formerly based primarily on craft skill” (176). This quote helped me understand that writing is not just recording practice. It transforms it. My game uses this by showing how choices to document, teach, or formalize a technique give the player new influence and legitimacy inside the game world. The game rewards the player for turning practice into knowledge because that is the transformation that Long describes.

I also learned from Agricola’s concern that too much knowledge was hidden. Long cites him saying it is shameful “that our things through our own negligence and idleness indeed now are almost concealed by darkness and lack their own ligh” (185). This quote helped me frame the problem the game explores. When players choose secrecy, the game world becomes harder, not easier. The game uses darkness and limited visibility to represent how hidden knowledge weakens communities.

My goal is for engineering students to see how openness, writing, and shared practice shaped technical fields long before modern engineering existed. The quotes from Long’s chapter guided all core design decisions.

Works Cited

Long, Pamela O. “Openness and Authorship I: Mining, Metallurgy, and the Military Arts.” Openness, Secrecy, Authorship: Technical Arts and the Culture of Knowledge, Johns Hopkins University Press, 2001, pp. 175–210.
